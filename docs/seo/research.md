# StraviFX SEO research notebook

The daily SEO task studies one topic each day and records what it learns
here. The **Playbook** is the distilled, current best practice; the task
reads it before building anything, so each day's research changes how the
next pages get built. The research log below it holds the raw findings and
sources.

## Playbook (read before every build; keep it under ~40 bullets)

Each bullet: the practice, why, and the source plus date it was confirmed.
Replace bullets that newer evidence contradicts instead of stacking them.

- Start definition and guide pages with a 40–60-word direct answer, written for the reader. Google says there is no need to chunk content or "write in a specific way just for generative AI search", so the answer is there because it helps people, not as an AI trick, and the rest of the page stays normal prose. ([Optimizing for generative AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide), updated 2026-07-10; the "AI lifts the first definition" part is still seeded)
- Keep the same name, description and logo on every profile, and list every profile in `SITE.sameAs`; consistent entity signals separate StraviFX from Strava and StriveFX. (Seeded 2026-10-15; to be confirmed)
- Bing's index feeds ChatGPT Search and Copilot; IndexNow speeds up Bing discovery. (Seeded 2026-10-15; to be confirmed)
- Finance is a YMYL topic: Google's raters expect YMYL pages to be highly accurate and consistent with expert consensus. Cite primary sources, keep risk disclosures visible, and show who publishes the site. ([Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content), updated 2026-10-05; the risk-disclosure part is still seeded)
- Every page needs original effort a reader can see: our own worked examples, an interactive tool, our own diagram or analysis. Raters score main content (which includes interactive tools) on effort, originality, skill and accuracy. Mass AI text without oversight counts as "little to no effort", citing others "doesn't replace the need for original effort", and Google has no preferred word count, so write to the question and never pad. ([Creating helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content), updated 2026-10-05; [changelog](https://developers.google.com/search/updates), 2026-10-01)
- Where a trading term has no standard definition (BOS confirmed by a close or a wick; what "MSS" means), say so on the page, name the common variants, and state which convention StraviFX uses. Ranking pages for these terms mostly present one version as fact. (Observed in SERPs, 2026-10-18; see research log)
- Diagrams go in as `<img>` files (SVG, WebP or PNG) with descriptive alt text and sit next to the text they explain. Google doesn't index CSS background images, and inline `<svg>` should carry a `<title>` for accessibility. ([Google image SEO best practices](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-images), updated 2026-03-02)
- Don't expect a Google rich result from FAQPage markup: Google stopped showing FAQ rich results on 2026-05-07 and removed the docs on 2026-06-15. Keep FAQPage only where a real, visible FAQ exists; never add an FAQ just for schema. ([Search Central changelog](https://developers.google.com/search/updates), 2026-05-08 and 2026-06-15)
- Skip HowTo markup. Google stopped showing HowTo rich results on 2023-09-13, so it adds work and no visibility. ([Changes to HowTo and FAQ rich results](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/08/howto-faq-changes), 2023-08-08, updated 2023-09-14)
- BreadcrumbList is still a supported rich result; keep it on every content page, with at least two items and the final item matching the page URL. ([Breadcrumb docs](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/breadcrumb), updated 2026-09-08)
- AI Overviews and AI Mode run on Google's core ranking systems: they need no llms.txt, AI text files, special markup or chunking, and unique, first-hand content matters most. Google's spam policies apply to AI responses too, and pages made "primarily to manipulate rankings or generative AI responses" count as scaled content abuse. Keep structured data that matches visible text (it still helps rich results); treat `llms.txt` as a cheap extra, never a priority. ([Optimizing for generative AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide), updated 2026-07-10; [changelog](https://developers.google.com/search/updates), 2026-05-15; [AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features), updated 2025-12-10)
- Never publish a self-ranking "best X" list (StraviFX at #1). In Google AI Overviews such pages are often cited while the publisher is left out of the recommendation, and ChatGPT's share of citations going to listicles roughly halved after GPT-5.6 (Aug 2026), with brands' own product pages gaining. For "best AI chart app"-type queries, publish a neutral buyer's guide with stated criteria and our own limits, and make our own product and feature pages the place that answers questions about StraviFX. (Evidence: strong, several independent trackers; see research log 2026-10-20)
- The SoftwareApplication/MobileApplication rich result requires `aggregateRating` or `review`. StraviFX has no first-party ratings to mark up, so the app schema stays rating-free (no rich result) until genuine on-site reviews exist; never borrow Play Store ratings. ([Software app docs](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/software-app), updated 2026-09-08; [review snippet guidelines on fake/incentivised reviews](https://developers.google.com/search/updates), 2026-07-24)

- Every date in structured data (`uploadDate`, `datePublished`, `dateModified`…) must be a full ISO 8601 datetime with a timezone, e.g. `2026-10-16T00:00:00+03:00` (Nairobi), never a bare `2026-10-16`. Search Console flagged the homepage VideoObject for exactly this. ([Video structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/video); Search Console Videos report, 2026-10-16)
- Never build links that Google's link spam policy names: keyword-rich, hidden or low-quality links inside widgets spread across sites, widely distributed footer/template links, or low-quality directory and bookmark links. If a calculator is ever offered as an embed, its credit link uses the brand name as anchor and `rel="nofollow"`. ([Spam policies: link spam](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies), updated 2026-08-28)
- Links mostly help Google discover pages and "happen naturally over time"; promotion through communities, social and word of mouth is fine, but overdoing it can harm the site. So every new page gets sitemap + internal links on day one, and off-site promotion stays an owner decision. ([SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide), updated 2025-12-10)
- Pages drafted by AI must be fact-checked before they ship, and so must their title, meta description, structured data and image alt text, because those show in results. Google's Oct 2026 guidance tells publishers to "manually factcheck and review all AI-generated content", and says using AI to make many pages without added value may be scaled content abuse. So every number gets a worked example, every regulatory or broker fact a cited primary source, and output stays one item per day. ([Using generative AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content), updated 2026-10-01; [Spam policies: scaled content abuse](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies), updated 2026-08-28)
- When terms overlap (market structure, swing points, HH/HL, BOS, CHoCH, MSS), give each page its own question to answer and link to its siblings instead of re-explaining them. Several of our pages showing for one query is not a problem in itself; unnecessary duplication and many weak pages are. Google's warning signs include "lots of content on many different topics" made in hope that some ranks. (Evidence: strong; John Mueller as reported by [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-answers-seo-question-about-keyword-cannibalization/556472/), 2025-09-22; [Creating helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content), updated 2026-10-05)

### 2026-10-21: SERP and competitor study: what ranks for "market structure shift", "swing high swing low" and "higher highs higher lows", and how should three overlapping glossary pages avoid competing with each other and with /glossary/market-structure?
- SERP check (US web search, 2026-10-21), "what is a market structure shift MSS in trading": TradingView script listings and education posts (many locale duplicates), LuxAlgo, FXOpen, an ICT Substack. (Evidence: strong, observed)
- Finding: the two strongest MSS pages define it as a reversal confirmation that needs displacement (a large candle closing through the level), with a liquidity sweep optional and a wick-only break not counting. FXOpen (about 2,300 words, 2026-05-29, no named author, 8-question FAQ) describes a failed swing (a lower high in an uptrend) followed by a displacement break, and calls a break without displacement "closer to a CHoCH". LuxAlgo (about 3,500 words, published 2025-08-12, shows "Reviewed by <name> on 2026-10-05") says CHoCH and MSS overlap, asks the reader to pick close or wick and test it, and uses a close in its example. Neither shows an annotated price chart. (Evidence: anecdotal for the definitions, two pages; strong that "MSS" is defined differently across sources, which matches the 2026-10-18 finding) [FXOpen: Market Structure Shift](https://fxopen.com/blog/en/market-structure-shift-meaning-and-use-in-ict-trading/), 2026-05-29; [LuxAlgo: MSS in ICT trading](https://www.luxalgo.com/blog/market-structure-shifts-mss-in-ict-trading/), 2025-08-12, reviewed 2026-10-05
- Finding: "swing high swing low how many candles" returns TradingView script listings and a Babypips forum thread where a beginner is confused because some articles say 3 candles and others 5; the answer given is that both are fine if used consistently. No page found answers "how many candles" with a worked example and the confirmation delay. (Evidence: strong, observed) [Babypips forum](https://forums.babypips.com/t/how-to-swing-trade/168573)
- Finding: "higher highs and higher lows uptrend definition" returns broker and education pages (Babypips Forexpedia, Gotrade, Rupeezy) that agree on the definition; the gap is labelling practice (LH+HL and HH+LL combinations, how an uptrend ends), not the definition. (Evidence: strong, observed) [Gotrade: Higher high explained](https://www.heygotrade.com/en/blog/higher-high-explained)
- Finding: John Mueller (Sept 2025) said several pages from one site showing for the same query isn't necessarily a problem, called "cannibalization" largely theoretical when Search Console doesn't show it, and advised reducing unnecessary duplication. (Evidence: strong, a Google employee's own words as reported by SEJ; not on a Google documentation page) [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-answers-seo-question-about-keyword-cannibalization/556472/), 2025-09-22. Google's helpful content page lists "producing lots of content on many different topics in hopes that some of it might perform well" as a search-engine-first warning sign. (Evidence: confirmed) [Creating helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content), updated 2026-10-05
- Applied today: each of the three new pages answers its own question (MSS: which meaning and what sequence; swings: how many candles and why they confirm late; HH/HL: how to label swings and read the combinations), and links to /glossary/market-structure for the overview instead of repeating it. The MSS page names the three meanings and states StraviFX's, consistent with the app glossary; the swing page states the app glossary's 1-candle rule and the Swing signals engine's 3-candles-each-side rule on the 4-hour chart (checked in the app's `swing-hunter/src/indicators.ts`; ties don't count).
- Rejected: separate pages for "MSS vs CHoCH", "bullish MSS", "bearish MSS", "3-candle swing" and "5-candle swing" to catch each phrasing (near-duplicate doorway pages; the questions are answered as sections and FAQs instead). Copying the "ICT" displacement thresholds from indicator scripts as if standard.
- Playbook change: added 1 bullet (overlapping terms: one question per page, link siblings).
- Proposal: Phase 6 item added to `docs/seo/plan.md` (glossary hub reading order).

### 2026-10-20: AI search (GEO): Q1: are self-published "best X" lists and comparison pages losing AI citations, and should StraviFX build "Best AI chart analysis apps for Android"?
- Finding: Google clarified on 2026-05-15 that its spam policies also apply to generative AI responses in Search, and published a guide the same day. The guide (updated 2026-07-10) says AI Overviews and AI Mode are "rooted in" core ranking systems; unique, first-hand content matters most; there is no need for llms.txt, AI text files, special markup, chunking or writing "just for generative AI search"; seeking "inauthentic 'mentions'" isn't as helpful as it seems; and making pages "primarily to manipulate rankings or generative AI responses" violates the scaled content abuse policy. It says nothing specific about listicles, "best" lists or comparisons. (Evidence: confirmed) [Search Central changelog](https://developers.google.com/search/updates), 2026-05-15; [Optimizing your website for generative AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide), updated 2026-07-10
- Finding: ChatGPT's share of retrieved pages that are listicles fell from about 15.5% to 8.2% after the GPT-5.6 rollout (August 2026), while first-party product pages rose to the largest share (16.4%), much of it from `site:`-scoped fan-out queries to brand domains. A separate study puts ChatGPT's first-party citation rate at 47.2% → 58.1%. Seer Interactive saw an earlier, smaller decline (listicle citations −30% month on month, Dec 2025 → Jan 2026, in 13 of 16 industries). (Evidence: strong for the direction, several independent trackers; the exact sizes vary by dataset) [Peec AI: GPT-5.6 product pages vs listicles](https://docs.peec.ai/research/gpt-56-product-pages-vs-listicles.md), 2026-08 (summarising David Konitzny's analysis); [Writesonic: GPT-5.6 citation study](https://writesonic.com/blog/gpt-5-6-sol-citation-study-vs-gpt-5-5), 2026; [Seer Interactive](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/the-listicle-window-is-closing-in-ai-search-30-decline-mom), 2026-02
- Finding: the specific "−50.5% listicles / −32.1% comparison pages" figures from the `ai-seo` skill could not be traced to a primary dataset; Peec's own page gives no comparison-page share and groups comparison pages with guides, which gained 20–50% in absolute daily retrievals. (Evidence: anecdotal for those exact numbers)
- Finding: the decline isn't universal: one tracker reported listicles as the fastest-growing ChatGPT citation format in July 2026, and a hotel-search study found listicle share up 7.6% after GPT-5.6. (Evidence: anecdotal, single studies) [Promptwatch](https://promptwatch.com/blog/are-listicles-still-relevant), 2026-07; [Nicolas Sitter](https://www.nicolassitter.com/research/gpt-5-6-hotel-search-changes-2026), 2026
- Finding: in Google AI Overviews for 100 B2B "best [category] software" queries (April to June 2026), when a brand's self-ranking listicle was cited, the brand was left out of the recommendation 69% of the time (224 of 323 citations); established brands were recommended instead. Google also demoted dozens of sites that leaned on such lists in organic results in January 2026. Other studies find a small lift from a cited self-ranking page (about 4% → 7% recommendation rate). (Evidence: anecdotal for the exact numbers, one engine and one vertical; strong for "a self-ranking list earns citations more than recommendations", which several sources agree on) [Lily Ray: Why calling yourself the best could be helping your competitors](https://lilyraynyc.substack.com/p/why-calling-yourself-the-best-could), 2026-06-17; [Search Engine Journal coverage](https://searchenginejournal.com/why-calling-yourself-the-best-could-be-helping-your-competitors-win-in-ai-search/579778/), 2026
- Finding: OpenAI's ChatGPT search surfaces sites through OAI-SearchBot and recommends allowing it in robots.txt; changes take about 24 hours. StraviFX's robots.txt allows every bot except on /learn/welcome, so nothing to change. (Evidence: confirmed) [OpenAI: Overview of OpenAI crawlers](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots), checked 2026-10-20
- Answer to Q1: the direction is confirmed by several independent trackers, though the exact percentages aren't. A StraviFX "best AI chart analysis apps" list ranking StraviFX would be a self-promotional listicle on a new, unknown domain: the profile most likely to be cited but not recommended, with organic demotion risk. Per the plan's rule, the Phase 4 item is replaced with a neutral buyer's guide.
- Rejected: building many "best X app for Y" listicles to catch fan-out queries (scaled content abuse per Google's 2026 guide); seeding "mentions" of StraviFX on other sites to influence AI answers (Google calls inauthentic mentions unhelpful; it would also be astroturfing).
- Playbook change: updated 2 bullets (direct answer is for readers; AI features run on core systems, spam policies apply to AI answers) and added 1 (no self-ranking "best X" lists).
- Proposal: none new. Q1 answered, so the Phase 4 "Best AI chart analysis apps for Android" item in `docs/seo/plan.md` was replaced with "How to choose an AI chart analysis app", as that item instructed.

### 2026-10-19: Google Search: what do Google's Aug–Oct 2026 changes (generative AI guidance, September spam update, preferred sources, Search profiles) mean for a new finance site whose pages are drafted by an AI agent?
- Finding: Google's generative AI guidance was rewritten on 2026-10-01 to match the rater guidelines. AI is fine for research and structure, but publishers should "manually factcheck and review all AI-generated content for accuracy and trustworthiness before publishing", including titles, meta descriptions, structured data and alt text. Using AI "to generate many pages without adding value for users" may violate the scaled content abuse policy. It suggests explaining how automation was used "in a way that makes sense for your audience". (Evidence: confirmed) [Using generative AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content), updated 2026-10-01; [Search Central changelog](https://developers.google.com/search/updates), 2026-10-01
- Finding: the helpful content page's "Who, How, and Why" section encourages accurate bylines that lead to more about the author, says AI or automation disclosures are useful where readers might wonder how content was made, and warns against deceptive authorship. (Evidence: confirmed) [Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content), updated 2026-10-05
- Finding: the scaled content abuse examples now explicitly list generative AI pages without added value, scraping, stitching content from other pages, and multiple sites hiding scale. (Evidence: confirmed) [Spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies), updated 2026-08-28
- Finding: 2026 ranking updates so far: March and May core updates; March, June, August and September spam updates; a February Discover update. Nothing was rolling out on 2026-10-19. The September spam update ran 2026-09-24 to about 10-08 (almost 14 days, longer than earlier 2026 spam updates); Google called it a "normal" spam update and named no target. (Evidence: confirmed for dates) [Search Status Dashboard: ranking history](https://status.search.google.com/products/rGHU1u87FJnkP6W2GwMi/history); (strong for "no named target") [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-september-2026-spam-update-done-rolling-out-493550), [Search Engine Roundtable](https://seroundtable.com/google-september-2026-spam-update-done-42235.html), 2026-10
- Finding: preferred sources (embeddable button since 2026-08-20) lets readers pick sites they want to see more of in Top Stories, and highlights them in AI Mode and AI Overviews; only domain or subdomain sites that appear in the source preferences tool qualify, and the button "is not required". (Evidence: confirmed) [Preferred sources](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/preferred-sources), updated 2026-09-18. StraviFX publishes no news and isn't indexed yet, so the button has little value now; revisit once Search Console shows indexed pages.
- Finding: Search profiles (badge docs added 2026-09-16) are reported to be US-only and to need about 100k followers on a major platform, so StraviFX isn't eligible. (Evidence: anecdotal, secondary blogs; the official eligibility page wasn't retrieved) [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-search-profile-badge-42106.html), [Similarweb](https://aisearch.similarweb.com/blog/google-search-profiles/), 2026
- Implication for StraviFX: the daily task ships AI-drafted YMYL pages with no human review, which is the exact case the 2026-10-01 guidance addresses. The safeguards already in the task (one item per day, worked-example math checks, cited sources, no invented stats) fit the guidance; the missing piece is a human fact-check and an honest "how this page was made" note, which are owner decisions.
- Rejected: none of the findings suggested grey-hat tactics. Adding a preferred-sources button now was considered and deferred (no news content, not indexed, adds third-party JavaScript).
- Playbook change: added 1 bullet (fact-check AI-drafted pages and their metadata; one item per day)
- Proposal: "Human fact-check and AI-assisted disclosure" added under Research proposals: owner decides in `docs/seo/plan.md`.

### 2026-10-18: Content and keyword opportunities: what do pages that rank for "break of structure" / "change of character" look like, and what gap can the StraviFX glossary fill?
- Finding: Google updated its helpful content guidance (changelog 2026-10-01, page updated 2026-10-05) with rater criteria for main content, which explicitly includes "interactive tools": effort, originality, talent or skill, and accuracy. YMYL topics, finances included, must be highly accurate and consistent with expert consensus. Generative AI text at scale "without manual oversight or curation represents little to no effort". "Attribution or giving credit to other sources doesn't replace the need for original effort." No preferred word count. (Evidence: confirmed) [Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content), updated 2026-10-05; [Search Central changelog](https://developers.google.com/search/updates), 2026-10-01
- Finding: Google Images indexes images only from `<img src>` (including inside `<picture>`), not CSS backgrounds; SVG is a supported format; alt text is the most important image metadata; images should sit near related text; inline SVG should use `<title>`. (Evidence: confirmed) [Google image SEO best practices](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-images), updated 2026-03-02
- SERP check (US web search, 2026-10-18) for "what is break of structure in trading", "break of structure vs change of character", "what is change of character CHoCH in forex" and "market structure shift vs change of character": results are mostly TradingView idea posts and indicator scripts (many locale duplicates), broker and exchange blogs (KuCoin, Gotrade, Phemex, EBC, Strike), the LuxAlgo concept library, ATAS, YouTube-summary sites, and scraped copies on unrelated hacked domains. (Evidence: strong, observed)
- Finding: the definitions disagree. Some sources confirm a BOS on a candle close and treat a wick as a liquidity grab, others accept a wick. "MSS" means a CHoCH to some authors, a stronger CHoCH with displacement after a sweep to others, and a continuation break to a few. (Evidence: strong, several independent sources) e.g. [Gotrade](https://heygotrade.com/en/blog/break-of-structure-bos-trading), [Trade The Pool](https://tradethepool.com/technical-skill/ict-market-structure-shift/), 2025, [Backtrex](https://backtrex.com/en/blog/ict-market-structure-beginner-guide), 2026
- Finding: LuxAlgo's Change of Character page (a strong library page) runs about 900–1,000 words of body text with a short definition, "how to identify", "how traders use it", "vs related concepts", related-concept links and a four-question FAQ, but no annotated chart, no author and no date. (Evidence: anecdotal, one page inspected) [LuxAlgo: Change of Character](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/change-of-character/)
- Likely follow-up questions, inferred from the results (no People Also Ask panel was visible): BOS vs CHoCH; should a break be confirmed by a close or a wick; is a CHoCH a reliable reversal signal; CHoCH vs MSS. (Evidence: anecdotal)
- Gap for StraviFX: an original annotated diagram of the swing points and the break, a page that names the competing conventions and states ours, the questions above answered directly, and links between the related terms. None of the pages checked did all four.
- Rejected: copying "success rate" figures from TradingView posts (e.g. a CHoCH "reaching 60%"); they are unsourced, and the brand rules forbid win-rate claims. Building one page per variant spelling (CHoCH / CHOCH / change of character): that would make near-duplicate doorway pages.
- Playbook change: added 3 bullets (original effort and no word-count target; name competing conventions; diagrams as `<img>` with alt text) and confirmed the YMYL bullet with a source.
- Proposal: Phase 6 item added to `docs/seo/plan.md` (visible publisher and last-updated line on content pages).

### 2026-10-17: Links and digital PR: how can StraviFX's free calculators earn links without breaking Google's link spam policy?
- Finding: Google's spam policy lists as link spam "keyword-rich, hidden, or low-quality links embedded in widgets that are distributed across various sites", widely distributed footer/template links, and low-quality directory or bookmark links. (Evidence: confirmed) [Spam policies for Google web search](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies), updated 2026-08-28
- Finding: Google's long-standing advice is to nofollow links inside widgets and embeds, and not to rely on them for links; sites ignoring it risked manual actions. (Evidence: strong, but dated) [Search Engine Land: Matt Cutts recommends nofollowing widget links](https://searchengineland.com/googles-matt-cutts-i-recommend-nofollowing-links-on-widgets-169487), 2013-08; [Search Engine Land: Google's stance on links within widgets](https://searchengineland.com/official-googles-latest-official-stance-on-links-within-widgets-181475), 2014-01
- Finding: `rel="sponsored"` for paid placements, `ugc` for user content, `nofollow` otherwise; Google may still crawl the target via sitemaps. (Evidence: confirmed) [Qualify your outbound links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/qualify-outbound-links), updated 2025-12-10
- Finding: Google says it primarily finds pages through links, that other sites linking to you "happens naturally over time", that promotion (social, community, word of mouth) speeds discovery, and that over-promoting can harm a site. (Evidence: confirmed) [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide), updated 2025-12-10
- Finding: Gary Illyes said in Sept 2023 that links are not a top-3 ranking signal and that ranking with zero links is possible. (Evidence: strong, several independent reports of the same talk, not on a Google page) e.g. [Outrank](https://www.outrank.co.uk/latest-news/are-links-still-the-kingpin-of-google-search-rankings/), [Footbridge Media](https://www.footbridgemedia.com/marketing-tips/did-google-say-links-dont-matter), 2023
