# StraviFX SEO research notebook

The daily SEO task studies one topic each day and records what it learns
here. The **Playbook** is the distilled, current best practice; the task
reads it before building anything, so each day's research changes how the
next pages get built. The research log below it holds the raw findings and
sources.

## Playbook (read before every build; keep it under ~40 bullets)

Each bullet: the practice, why, and the source plus date it was confirmed.
Replace bullets that newer evidence contradicts instead of stacking them.

- Start definition and guide pages with a 40–60-word direct answer, written for the reader. Google says there is no need to chunk content or "write in a specific way just for generative AI search", so the answer is there because it helps people, not as an AI trick, and the rest of the page stays normal prose. ([Optimizing for generative AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide), updated 2026-07-10; the "AI lifts the first definition" part is still seeded)
- Keep the same name, description and logo on every profile, and list every profile in `SITE.sameAs`; consistent entity signals separate StraviFX from Strava and StriveFX. (Seeded 2026-10-15; to be confirmed)
- Bing's index feeds ChatGPT Search and Copilot; IndexNow speeds up Bing discovery. (Seeded 2026-10-15; to be confirmed)
- Finance is a YMYL topic: Google's raters expect YMYL pages to be highly accurate and consistent with expert consensus. Cite primary sources, keep risk disclosures visible, and show who publishes the site. ([Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content), updated 2026-10-05; the risk-disclosure part is still seeded)
- Every page needs original effort a reader can see: our own worked examples, an interactive tool, our own diagram or analysis. Raters score main content (which includes interactive tools) on effort, originality, skill and accuracy. Mass AI text without oversight counts as "little to no effort", citing others "doesn't replace the need for original effort", and Google has no preferred word count, so write to the question and never pad. ([Creating helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content), updated 2026-10-05; [changelog](https://developers.google.com/search/updates), 2026-10-01)
- Where a trading term has no standard definition (BOS confirmed by a close or a wick; what "MSS" means), say so on the page, name the common variants, and state which convention StraviFX uses. Ranking pages for these terms mostly present one version as fact. (Observed in SERPs, 2026-10-18; see research log)
- Diagrams go in as `<img>` files (SVG, WebP or PNG) with descriptive alt text and sit next to the text they explain. Google doesn't index CSS background images, and inline `<svg>` should carry a `<title>` for accessibility. ([Google image SEO best practices](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-images), updated 2026-03-02)
- Don't expect a Google rich result from FAQPage markup: Google stopped showing FAQ rich results on 2026-05-07 and removed the docs on 2026-06-15. Keep FAQPage only where a real, visible FAQ exists; never add an FAQ just for schema. ([Search Central changelog](https://developers.google.com/search/updates), 2026-05-08 and 2026-06-15)
- Skip HowTo markup. Google stopped showing HowTo rich results on 2023-09-13, so it adds work and no visibility. ([Changes to HowTo and FAQ rich results](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/08/howto-faq-changes), 2023-08-08, updated 2023-09-14)
- BreadcrumbList is still a supported rich result; keep it on every content page, with at least two items and the final item matching the page URL. ([Breadcrumb docs](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/breadcrumb), updated 2026-09-08)
- AI Overviews and AI Mode run on Google's core ranking systems: they need no llms.txt, AI text files, special markup or chunking, and unique, first-hand content matters most. Google's spam policies apply to AI responses too, and pages made "primarily to manipulate rankings or generative AI responses" count as scaled content abuse. Keep structured data that matches visible text (it still helps rich results); treat `llms.txt` as a cheap extra, never a priority. ([Optimizing for generative AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide), updated 2026-07-10; [changelog](https://developers.google.com/search/updates), 2026-05-15; [AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features), updated 2025-12-10)
- Never publish a self-ranking "best X" list (StraviFX at #1). In Google AI Overviews such pages are often cited while the publisher is left out of the recommendation, and ChatGPT's share of citations going to listicles roughly halved after GPT-5.6 (Aug 2026), with brands' own product pages gaining. For "best AI chart app"-type queries, publish a neutral buyer's guide with stated criteria and our own limits, and make our own product and feature pages the place that answers questions about StraviFX. (Evidence: strong, several independent trackers; see research log 2026-10-20)
- The SoftwareApplication/MobileApplication rich result requires `aggregateRating` or `review`. StraviFX has no first-party ratings to mark up, so the app schema stays rating-free (no rich result) until genuine on-site reviews exist; never borrow Play Store ratings. ([Software app docs](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/software-app), updated 2026-09-08; [review snippet guidelines on fake/incentivised reviews](https://developers.google.com/search/updates), 2026-07-24)

- Every date in structured data (`uploadDate`, `datePublished`, `dateModified`…) must be a full ISO 8601 datetime with a timezone, e.g. `2026-10-16T00:00:00+03:00` (Nairobi), never a bare `2026-10-16`. Search Console flagged the homepage VideoObject for exactly this. ([Video structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/video); Search Console Videos report, 2026-10-16)
- Never build links that Google's link spam policy names: keyword-rich, hidden or low-quality links inside widgets spread across sites, widely distributed footer/template links, or low-quality directory and bookmark links. If a calculator is ever offered as an embed, its credit link uses the brand name as anchor and `rel="nofollow"`. ([Spam policies: link spam](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies), updated 2026-08-28)
- Links mostly help Google discover pages and "happen naturally over time"; promotion through communities, social and word of mouth is fine, but overdoing it can harm the site. So every new page gets sitemap + internal links on day one, and off-site promotion stays an owner decision. ([SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide), updated 2025-12-10)
- Pages drafted by AI must be fact-checked before they ship, and so must their title, meta description, structured data and image alt text, because those show in results. Google's Oct 2026 guidance tells publishers to "manually factcheck and review all AI-generated content", and says using AI to make many pages without added value may be scaled content abuse. So every number gets a worked example, every regulatory or broker fact a cited primary source, and output stays one item per day. ([Using generative AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content), updated 2026-10-01; [Spam policies: scaled content abuse](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies), updated 2026-08-28)
- When terms overlap (market structure, swing points, HH/HL, BOS, CHoCH, MSS), give each page its own question to answer and link to its siblings instead of re-explaining them. Several of our pages showing for one query is not a problem in itself; unnecessary duplication and many weak pages are. Google's warning signs include "lots of content on many different topics" made in hope that some ranks. (Evidence: strong; John Mueller as reported by [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-answers-seo-question-about-keyword-cannibalization/556472/), 2025-09-22; [Creating helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content), updated 2026-10-05)
- Play listing copy (drafted for the owner, never published by the task) follows Google Play's Metadata policy: no ranking or performance claims ("#1", "Popular", "App of the year"), no price or promo text in the title or icon, no unattributed or anonymous testimonials in the description, no repetitive or unrelated keywords. Google's own discovery page names the title and description as the searchable listing text; reviews and replies are not documented as indexed, so never plan copy around them. ([Metadata policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898842); [Get discovered on Google Play search](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/4448378), both checked 2026-10-22)
- Write everything an AI answer might quote as visible page text, and treat JSON-LD as a helper for Google rich results and Bing's understanding, not as a way to get cited. Adding JSON-LD made no measurable difference to ChatGPT or Google AI Mode citations in a matched study of 1,885 pages, and AI systems fetching a page live read the visible HTML and skipped JSON-LD. Microsoft says schema helps Bing and Copilot understand content. Nothing shows FAQPage markup helps with ChatGPT, Perplexity or Copilot, so the FAQ stays visible, and the markup stays only where that visible FAQ exists. Keep JSON-LD in the server-rendered HTML, as `ContentPage` does now. (Evidence: strong; [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/schema-ai-citations/), 2026-05-11; Fabrice Canel at SMX Munich, March 2025, as reported by [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/microsoft-bing-copilot-use-schema-for-its-llms-453455); research log 2026-10-23)
- Guides answer the follow-up questions a reader would search next, in the page itself: Google says AI Overviews and AI Mode may issue "multiple related searches across subtopics and data sources" (query fan-out) and pick supporting pages that are indexed and snippet-eligible. Find the follow-ups from forum threads and the gaps in ranking pages, answer them as normal sections or a visible FAQ, and never split them into thin pages of their own. ([AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features), updated 2025-12-10; research log 2026-10-25)

### 2026-10-25: Content and keyword opportunities: what do people ask about setting a stop loss and the 1% risk rule, and what do ranking pages miss, so the first two Learn guides fill a real gap?
- Finding: Google documents that AI Overviews and AI Mode "may use a 'query fan-out' technique", issuing multiple related searches across subtopics and data sources, and that a page qualifies as a supporting link if it is indexed and eligible for a snippet; nothing else is required. (Evidence: confirmed) [AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features), updated 2025-12-10. Counts of sub-queries per question (8–12) are third-party estimates. (Evidence: anecdotal) e.g. [Aleyda Solis](https://www.aleydasolis.com/en/ai-search/google-query-fan-out/)
- SERP check (US web search, 2026-10-25), "how to set a stop loss and take profit in forex for beginners": Babypips forum threads, broker academies (FxPro, BlackBull, VT Markets) and an MQL5 forum thread. Broker pages give a fixed-pip example (buy at 1.1000, 50-pip stop) and a 1:2 rule of thumb, and mention structure and ATR in a sentence. (Evidence: strong, observed)
- Finding: the strongest independent stop-loss guide found (Trade That Swing, 2025-11-05, about 2,000 words, named CMT author, six annotated charts, seven numeric examples including 1×/1.5×/2× ATR stops) has no formula linking the stop distance to position size, no FAQ, and lists position size before the stop in one place and after it in another. (Evidence: anecdotal, one page inspected) [Trade That Swing](https://tradethatswing.com/how-and-where-to-place-stop-loss-orders-on-your-forex-trades), 2025-11-05
- Finding: sources disagree on the order of the two decisions. Some say pick the risk first and let it set the stop distance; others (and the 1% rule pages) say place the stop where the idea is wrong, then size the position from it. (Evidence: strong, observed in several results) e.g. [CrossTrade: the 1% rule](https://crosstrade.io/learn/risk-management/one-percent-rule), 2026-04-14. StraviFX's convention, already in the lot size calculator page and the app's level rules ("never shrink the stop"), is structure first, then size; the guide should say so and name the other view.
- Finding: the clearest 1% rule page (CrossTrade, about 1,100 words, 4-question FAQ) explains "1% is the loss at the stop, not the position size" and shows losing-streak math (10 losses: about 9.6% down at 1%, 18% at 2%, 40% at 5%; checked: 0.99^10 = 0.904, 0.98^10 = 0.817, 0.95^10 = 0.599) but uses only futures contracts, no forex lots or pips. (Evidence: anecdotal, one page) [CrossTrade](https://crosstrade.io/learn/risk-management/one-percent-rule), 2026-04-14
- Finding: small accounts hit a wall the ranking pages skip: with a 0.01-lot minimum, 1% of $50 ($0.50) allows only a 5-pip EUR/USD stop, and a 20-pip stop risks $2 (4%); $100 allows 10 pips. Forum threads and broker blogs raise it; no ranking guide works it through. (Evidence: strong for the question being asked, several forum threads; the math is checked here) e.g. [Babypips forum](https://forums.babypips.com/t/1-of-deposit-metatrader/125617); [TopBrokers: position sizing](https://topbrokers.com/forex-basics/position-sizing-in-forex/). Our lot size calculator already says when the size falls below 0.01 lots, but not what 0.01 lots would risk or the widest stop that fits.
- Finding: questions on ATR multipliers (1.5× vs 2×) and moving a stop to break-even are answered only by indicator scripts and coding forums with no tested evidence either way. (Evidence: anecdotal) e.g. [ProRealCode](https://www.prorealcode.com/topic/stop-loss-scale-out-and-trailing-stop-using-the-atr/). A guide can explain the trade-off but must not recommend a multiplier as proven.
- Answer: the gap for the two Learn guides is (1) a worked chain from chart structure to stop to lot size with the formula on the page, (2) forex-specific 1% math with lots and pips, including the small-account case at 0.01 lots, and (3) naming the "risk first vs stop first" disagreement and stating StraviFX's convention. The follow-up questions to answer in-page: where exactly beyond the level (spread, ATR buffer), what if the stop is too wide for 1%, is 2% okay, what happens to the stop in a news gap, should I move it to break-even.
- Rejected: separate thin pages for "1% rule for $50 account", "1% rule for $100 account", etc. (near-duplicate doorway pages; the guide covers them as one table); quoting win rates or "professional traders risk X%" figures from unsourced blogs.
- Playbook change: added 1 bullet (answer the follow-up questions in the page; query fan-out is documented).
- Proposal: Phase 6 item added to `docs/seo/plan.md` (lot size calculator: what 0.01 lots would risk, and the widest stop that fits).

### 2026-10-23: Technical SEO: Q3: do ChatGPT search, Perplexity and Copilot use FAQPage or other schema when choosing what to cite?
- Finding: Ahrefs ran a matched difference-in-differences study of 1,885 pages that added JSON-LD between August 2025 and March 2026, against about 4,000 controls, measuring citations for 30 days before and after. ChatGPT +2.2% and Google AI Mode +2.4%, neither distinguishable from zero; Google AI Overviews −4.6% (significant but small, and unexplained). Schema types were pooled (FAQPage not tested on its own); Perplexity and Copilot weren't covered; every page was already heavily cited, so it says nothing about new pages like ours. Conclusion: "JSON-LD isn't going to be the unlock". (Evidence: strong; one well-designed study from a reputable source, consistent with the searchVIU test below and with Google's own docs) [Ahrefs: Does adding schema increase AI citations?](https://ahrefs.com/blog/schema-ai-citations/), 2026-05-11; coverage: [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/schema-markup-didnt-move-ai-citations-in-ahrefs-test/574568/), 2026-05
- Finding: in a searchVIU test (October 2025), ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini and Google AI Mode were asked to fetch a page live; they extracted the visible HTML and ignored JSON-LD, hidden Microdata and hidden RDFa. It tested only live fetching, not indexing or training. (Evidence: anecdotal; the original write-up wasn't retrieved, only secondary summaries, including Ahrefs citing it) [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/schema-ai-citations/), 2026-05-11; [iloveseo.net](https://www.iloveseo.net/the-ahrefs-schema-study-is-right-and-its-testing-the-wrong-thing), 2026
- Finding: Microsoft's Fabrice Canel said at SMX Munich (March 2025) that schema markup helps Microsoft's LLMs understand content for Bing and Copilot. No Microsoft documentation page saying this was found, and nobody has measured the effect on Copilot citations. (Evidence: strong that it was said, several independent reports; anecdotal for any effect on citations) [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/microsoft-bing-copilot-use-schema-for-its-llms-453455), 2025-03
- Finding: Perplexity's crawler documentation says PerplexityBot exists to "surface and link websites in search results" and is not used for model training, and that Perplexity-User fetches pages for a user's question and generally ignores robots.txt. It says nothing about structured data or how citations are chosen. Vendor claims that FAQPage schema raises Perplexity citations (e.g. "89% higher citation probability") cite no data. (Evidence: confirmed for the crawler facts; anecdotal for the schema claims) [Perplexity crawlers](https://docs.perplexity.ai/guides/bots), no date, checked 2026-10-23; e.g. [pixelmojo](https://www.pixelmojo.io/answers/how-do-i-get-cited-by-perplexity)
- Finding: correlational studies disagree with the Ahrefs result (AirOps: 38.5% vs 32.0% ChatGPT citation rate with vs without JSON-LD; a UC Berkeley "GEO-16" paper rates structured data as a factor; an OtterlyAI rollout saw Google AI Overview gains, drops on ChatGPT, Gemini and Copilot, no change on Perplexity). Correlation can't separate schema from the broader SEO work that tends to come with it, which is the point of Ahrefs' matched design. (Evidence: anecdotal, via a secondary summary) [Analyzify research roundup](https://analyzify.com/hub/schema-markup-ai-citations-research), 2026
- Finding: AI crawlers are reported not to run JavaScript, so JSON-LD injected on the client would be invisible to them. StraviFX pages are prerendered and the JSON-LD is in the server HTML (checked with curl today), so nothing to fix. (Evidence: anecdotal) [OpenReplay](https://blog.openreplay.com/json-ld-ai-understand-website/), 2026
- Answer to Q3: there is no evidence that ChatGPT search or Perplexity use FAQPage or other schema to choose citations, and the best controlled evidence finds no measurable lift for ChatGPT. Copilot may get some benefit indirectly through Bing's understanding of the page, per Microsoft, but that is unmeasured. Practical upshot for StraviFX: no change to what we ship (BreadcrumbList, DefinedTerm, FAQPage only over a visible FAQ). The value is in the visible text: the direct answer, the worked example and the FAQ answers themselves.
- Rejected: adding FAQ sections or FAQPage markup to pages without a real reader question, to chase AI citations (no evidence it works, and Google treats markup that doesn't match visible content as a structured-data policy problem); hiding text in JSON-LD that isn't on the page (live AI fetches skip it anyway, and it would break the "matches visible content" rule).
- Playbook change: added 1 bullet (AI citations come from visible text; JSON-LD is for Google rich results and Bing understanding).
- Proposal: none. The site already does what the evidence supports. The Bing Webmaster Tools import already in "Blocked: needs the owner" is the step that would let us check Bing/Copilot directly.

### 2026-10-22: Google Play ASO: Q2: does Google Play index review text and developer replies for search, and what should StraviFX do about it?
- Finding: Google's own help page for Play search says apps are ranked on "a combination of ratings, reviews, downloads, and other factors" (weights proprietary) and names the title and description as the listing text that helps users find an app. It mentions reviews only as a ranking signal, not as searchable text, and recommends responding to users and addressing issues. (Evidence: confirmed) [Get discovered on Google Play search](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/4448378), no date shown, checked 2026-10-22
- Finding: one controlled experiment (RadASO "ASO Mythbusters", Ukrainian apps, tests run late October to late December, year not stated) found Google Play indexed keywords from review text (16 of 33 longer reviews; 0 of 10 reviews of five words or fewer) and from two developer replies, usually within 2–10 days and mostly at low positions; App Store reviews were not indexed; review likes and featured status made no difference. (Evidence: anecdotal, a single vendor experiment with small samples and no publication date) [RadASO: Do reviews get indexed in the App Store and Google Play?](https://radaso.com/asomythbusters-experiments/do-reviews-get-indexed-in-the-app-store-and-google-play)
- Finding: the vendor blogs that repeat "Play indexes replies" trace back to that one experiment; Reply Argus (a reply-tool vendor) calls it "a working theory, not a published ranking factor" and warns that keyword-stuffed replies risk policy action. AppFollow says replies improve satisfaction and ratings but don't move keyword rankings. (Evidence: anecdotal, not independent) [Reply Argus](https://www.replyargus.com/blog/google-play-indexes-review-replies-aso), 2026-06-28; [AppFollow: top 10 ASO myths](https://appfollow.io/blog/busting-the-top-10-myths)
- Finding: Google Play's Metadata policy bans ranking/performance claims ("#1", "Popular"), price or promo text in the title and icon, unattributed or anonymous testimonials in the description, and repetitive or unrelated keywords; the title is limited to 30 characters. The Ratings, Reviews and Installs policy bans incentivised or fake reviews and forced prompts, and advises replies that address the user's issue and never ask for a higher rating. (Evidence: confirmed) [Metadata](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898842); [User Ratings, Reviews, and Installs](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898684), checked 2026-10-22
- Finding: Google's Play Console marketing page says developers can "create up to 50 custom store listings tailored by country, user or buyer state, or search keyword", and review which keywords people use to find the app. No eligibility rules are stated there. (Evidence: confirmed that the feature exists; eligibility unknown) [Play Console: store listings](https://google.play/business/storelistings/), checked 2026-10-22
- Answer to Q2: possibly yes for both, but only one small, undated vendor experiment supports it and Google doesn't document it. Not adopted into the Playbook. Practical upshot: reply to reviews because it helps users and ratings (which Google does name as a factor); if a reply naturally uses the reviewer's own words ("chart analysis", "lot size"), that's fine, but write every reply as if indexing didn't exist.
- Rejected: asking users to mention particular words in their reviews (review manipulation; it also fails the "would you do this if no search engine existed" test); keyword lists or copy-pasted blocks in replies (keyword spam under the Metadata policy); seeding reviews from staff or friends (fake reviews).
